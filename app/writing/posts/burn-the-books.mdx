# Burn the Books

Reading isn’t special. Everyone says that they should read more. People should read more. Reading makes you a better person, somehow. Okay. What specifically is so great about this? Let’s look at the top 10 books that people are reading:

1. “self-help” AKA I don’t know how to take care of myself so I must draw from an endless supply of trite inspiration to motivate me to take one shaky impermanent step in a good direction. If these actually worked then new ones would not keep getting published.
2. “word porn” AKA my 3 pets and cappucino addiction don’t excite my solitary life enough (shocker) so I feed my sexual appetite with corny idealizations of who I could have been in my twenties
3. “fantasy” AKA I can’t bear to live in this world so it’s better I curl up inside some soft, comfortable, dreamy contrivance and if the real world was like that ohhh gosh I would rule
4. “the world sucks” AKA I need coping mechanism to deal with the fact that my overall emotional state is a reaction to my surroundings, and being depressively “informed” will somehow make me intellectually richer 

These are not beneficial.

Some of the most idiotic societal tenets have emerged from the notion that one must be taught how to be a human rather than simply discover it.

I used to be really really sanctimonious about reading a lot of books. Some people would say “wow you’re so smart” and i’d be like “yeah i just read a lot of books, i guess”. But reading is a symptom of good qualities much more than it is a cause. 

A lot of people read and get dumber. There are a lot of really fucking bad books out there that caused a lot of damage to humanity… like Mein Kampf or the Annals of the Elders of Zion or the 50 Shades of Grey. If certain specific books hadn’t been published and read widely, the world would actually be a much better, more harmonious place.

Some people read and then get smarter, but somehow it makes them worse humans overall. Like people who read about how to manipulate others… things like *The Prince* or *The Game* or *Influence* or *48 Laws of Power.* I’d much, much rather live in a world in which no one has read those books than in a world in which everyone has.

When I was little, I remember forcing myself to read. My grandfather got me *Tom Sawyer* when I was like 11 and I actually enjoyed it a lot. And then he gifted me with 20,000 Leagues Under the Sea, citing its literary virtuosity or something, and reading it felt to me like sisyphus and syphilis at the same time. I don’t know how I managed to bear the first 45% of the book, yet of course in every conversation I had with my grandpa I acted as if it was the most glorious thing I’d ever experienced. I certainly felt smart in front of my so-called middle school friends, reading a fancy old leather-bound book, built-in bookmark ribbon and all. Now that I think about it, I don’t think he ever even read the fucking book. 

I don’t remember anything from Tom Sawyer.

I hate it when people say OH MY GOD YOU HAVE TO READ THIS. So like I haven’t truly lived until I’ve consumed some information that some probably depressed, definitely pretentious dude vomited in the duration of one of his less manic mental states. There might be something to say for the ability of psychopathological people to convey human experiences in compelling ways. But most of it is just blabber. Gibberish. Utter yappery.

You walk into a bookstore and it’s louder than a Persian bat-mitzvah with uncle Faraj’s DJ set. It’s louder than a jet engine, the atmosphere polluted with the words of people who haven’t quite figured it out for themselves telling you how to life your life. Each cover claims it will be the one. “Free Up Your Soul!” **How to Finally Let Go.** THIS ONE BOOK Will Change Your Life. It’s all you need, really. These 45,000 words will be your personal bible, telling you when to talk to her and if you should break up with him and what to do about your fraught relationship with your mother and how to never give up. Like, shut the fuck up. The power of advice is the most overestimated feature of modernity.

Books, actually, should be judged by their covers much more than they commonly are. The cover can tell you a lot—what the book is about, for example. Or what genre it is. “A novel” is pretty fucking descriptive. Good covers sell, especially when people are reading performatively in the first place. The performance is not always a boast; it may be internal; it’s self-performative. Proving to myself that *I* am a reader!! Genuinely, how else are you supposed to figure out which book to read? Should you take a recommendation—take someone else’s bad advice for which particular other piece of bad advice you should consume? Certainly you should follow your heart. And you can’t judge a book by its words—because by then it’s already stolen your time. And if a book says on the cover that it’ll change your life you can be pretty damn sure it’s not going to.

Maybe the worst is the bestsellers section. People read to discover themselves or something, right? To be unique? At least that’s what that one guy sitting in the corner the raging bar on a Saturday behind his fresh stack of tree pulp believes. If everyone reads the same book, it’s as if no one even read the book at all. The book might as well have never existed in the first place. So why bother?

The fact that something exists does not imply that it should be experienced. Just cause someone said something doesn’t mean you should listen. *Maybe* you should. But more likely, in the futile search for your own overarching philosophy, you’ll find yourself trapped inside someone else’s flawed one. 

Oh, and don’t even get me started about the loneliness epidemic.
